1. Install pprl

Install uv, then pprl 3.0.0:

uv tool install https://github.com/TuftsCTSI/pprl/releases/download/v3.0.0/pprl-3.0.0-py3-none-any.whl

Open a new terminal, and run pprl: it lists its commands. If a step fails, or your computer restricts what you can install, write to Jo Example (jo@example.org).

2. Create your workspace

You receive your bundle, site_a.pprl, from Jo Example, over a secure channel. It holds your role's settings: never forward it, and never open it.

Your workspace is a new, empty folder you create, such as example-project-site_a, that will hold your patients' details until the end of the project. It must be somewhere your organization has approved for them, on an encrypted drive, where files can be deleted permanently. Keep it out of search indexing, cloud sync and backups. Put the bundle in the folder, open a terminal there, and run:

pprl init

pprl reads your role and site from the bundle and shows them back, makes the folder your workspace, makes your key pair, and lists the files it created. Open a terminal in your workspace for every step below.

Every file in this folder is deleted at the end of the project, so keep nothing else here, such as your notes.

Open no file in your workspace but site_a.set_aside.csv (step 7), not even to look: an editor, a spreadsheet or your computer itself can leave copies of a file elsewhere on the disk, such as backup and AutoRecover files or a search index, which pprl cannot delete. To see what a file holds, run pprl inspect on it, such as pprl inspect outbox/linkage/site_a.hashes.pprl.

4. Export your tables

Export each of the project's tables, with one row for each record, keyed by your own ids, and save it in your workspace's tables/ folder, as CSV: in Excel, choose File > Save As > "CSV UTF-8". Leave a cell empty where the value is unknown (NULL), and close the file once it is saved.

  • tables/scores.csv: exactly the columns person_id and score, in any order; one row for each person_id. pprl select keeps the rows where score is not empty.

5. Select the study's rows

pprl select

pprl checks each table, keeps only the rows the project's rules keep, and says how many rows each rule left out. It writes site_a.selected.pprl, which stays in your workspace and is never sent. The rows it leaves out are not written anywhere. Its patients are the study's: only they are hashed.

Read its summary, and do what any warning says.

6. Export your patient file

Export one row for each of your patients, as your records identify them, keyed by the same ids as your tables, with exactly these columns, named exactly so (names are case-sensitive), and no others. pprl leaves out every patient without a row that pprl select kept, so the file may hold more patients than the study's.

Save it in your workspace as patients.csv, as CSV UTF-8, and close it. pprl reads any line endings, and a file saved as Windows text.

7. Clean your patient file

pprl clean

pprl leaves out the patients who are not the study's, checks the rest, repairs what it safely can, and gives each patient a random study id in place of your own id. It writes site_a.clean.pprl, which stays in your workspace and is never sent, and site_a.set_aside.csv, the rows it could not use: each one's row number in patients.csv, your id, and the reason, and none of the patient's details, so that your data team can find and fix the record in your own database. It is the one file in your workspace to open.

Read its warnings, and do what each says.

8. Hash your patients

pprl hash

pprl unlocks the secret, turns each patient's identifying details in site_a.clean.pprl into a hash with it, and writes outbox/linkage/site_a.hashes.pprl: each patient's study id and hash, and nothing else.

Read its summary: it hashes every patient pprl clean kept, and no others. If it shows a warning, do what the warning says.

9. Pack your tables

pprl pack

pprl checks the rows pprl select kept against the project's rules again, and writes outbox/merge/site_a.tables.pprl: those rows, with each of your ids replaced by its study id. It stays in your workspace until the go-ahead (step 11). You pack now, before your hashes leave, so that any problem with your patients or tables comes to light first. It leaves out, with a warning, the rows of a patient it has no study id for: one pprl clean set aside, or one not in your patient file.

Read its warnings, and do what each says.

Then, in your workspace, run:

pprl lists every file in your workspace, asks you to type "yes", and deletes them permanently. It leaves the folder, with only your receipt, outbox/coordinator/site_a.receipt_<date>.txt: send it to Jo Example over a secure channel. The receipt holds your workspace's site_a.log, and no patient details or secret. You may then delete the folder.

pprl destroy cannot delete copies kept by your storage (recycle bins, version history, retention policies and backups; OneDrive keeps deleted files for up to 93 days), so follow these up. Nor can it delete files you sent on, such as hashes at the linkage agent: each recipient destroys the copies received, and confirms that. Your drive's encryption protects data left on the drive after deletion.
