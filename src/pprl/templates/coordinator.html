You are the coordinator of $project: you choose its settings, write every role's bundle and guide, and send them. Every other role follows a guide written for that role, which you send with the role's bundle. This one stays in your workspace, and is never sent.

You are also one of the sites. Your site's workspace is a separate folder, and you follow your site's guide as any site participant does, holding back your site's tables until the go-ahead too.

Through your site you hold the project's secret, so you must never have access to another site's hashes: never receive one, nor set up a place to share them or the linkage file. That would in effect give you that site's patients' details. So someone else holds the linkage agent's role, and no site participant holds that role or the merge agent's.

1. Install pprl

Install uv, then pprl $release:

$install

Open a new terminal, and run pprl: it lists its commands.

2. Your workspace and the project's settings

Your workspace is a new, empty folder, such as $folder, that holds the project's settings and every bundle until the end of the project: it must be somewhere your organization has approved for it, on an encrypted drive, left out of search indexing, cloud sync and backups.

In it, write $project_file, the project's settings: its sites, each site's id column, the fields to hash and their weights, the threshold, and how each table is merged. The order of its sites breaks every tie in the merge: put the definitive site first, the one whose records for the study's tables are the most complete and current (then the enrolling site, then the one with the most patients), and note why beside it. Then open a terminal in the folder, and run:

$init

It makes the folder your workspace, for a new project. Open a terminal in your workspace for every step below.

$deleted_at_end

To see what a bundle holds, run pprl inspect on it.

3. The bundles and the guides

Choose the fields' weights, the hash length, the fill ratio and the threshold on synthetic data, with $synth and $sweep. Then write every bundle:

$bundles

$deployment_file, beside $project_file, holds what the guides tell each role: whom to write to, and the times. Then write every guide:

$guides

It writes a guide for each recipient into the recipient's folder in $outbox, beside the bundle, and this one, $own_guide. Read each before you send it.

4. Send the bundles and guides

Send each recipient exactly the files in the recipient's folder in your $outbox, over a secure channel, planned for around $time_bundles:

$sends
ToContactFiles

A site's bundle holds the fields' weights: it goes to that site's participant only. Your own site's needs no sending: copy it between your two workspaces.

5. Send each site participant the secret

Once the site workspace exists, each site participant sends you the site's public key over a secure channel. Accept a public key only from the site participant you already know: pprl has no other check of whose it is. Save each in your workspace under its own name: $keys. Then run:

$secrets

It makes the project's one secret, locks it to each site's public key, and keeps no copy: this workspace cannot read it. It writes $secret_files. Then send each site participant that site's file over a secure channel, planned for around $time_secrets:

$secret_sends
ToContactFile

Your own site's public key and secret file don't need sending: copy each between your two workspaces.

6. Review the linkage

The site participants send the hashes to the linkage agent, and keep the tables back. The linkage agent sends the linkage file to the merge agent, and you the linkage report, $linkage_report, expected around $time_link: what pprl link found, as counts only, with no record and no hash. Save it in your workspace, under that name, without opening it, and run:

$review

It shows what the linkage report holds, and writes a histogram of best scores beside it, $histogram. Leave both in your workspace until the end of the project, when $destroy deletes them (step 8). Judge from them whether the linkage is plausible:

  • each pair of sites: same secret, or not checked, and why (too few records, or $project_file turns the check off);
  • linked pairs near the overlap you expect;
  • a clear gap at the threshold, with few near misses;
  • an estimate of false pairs small enough for the study;
  • each warning from pprl link understood.

If it is plausible, tell the linkage agent, $contact_linkage, to delete every copy of the hashes, destroy the linkage workspace and send you the receipt. Save the receipt in your workspace, and read it: does anything in it suggest a problem with pprl, such as a step that was hard to follow or a bug worth reporting?

7. Send the go-ahead

Once you hold the linkage agent's receipt, and not before, tell every site participant and the merge agent to go ahead. This is planned for around $time_review. The go-ahead means that the linkage agent's hashes are gone before any table moves. Each site participant then sends the site's tables, and the merge agent starts merging. Site participants keep every other file in the site workspaces until the end of the project (step 8).

8. At the end of the project

When the host confirms receipt of the merged dataset, expected around $time_merge, tell every site participant and the merge agent that the project's transfers are complete, and that the workspaces may be destroyed. No role destroys a workspace before you say so. Each deletes any copy shared outside the workspace, destroys the workspace and sends you the receipt. This is planned for around $time_destroy. Save each receipt you receive in your workspace, and read it, as in step 6. Delete any copy of the bundles and guides you shared. Then, in your workspace, run:

$destroy

It deletes every file in your workspace, including the linkage report, its histogram and the receipts you received, none of which is needed after the project, and leaves the folder, with only your receipt in it, $receipt.

$destroy_limits

Questions

Every role writes to you with questions: welcome each one, however small, as a step one role found unclear may trouble others too. When pprl's message doesn't settle a problem, the role sends you the workspace's log over a secure channel: it holds what pprl showed, and no patient details or secret.
